One pulse, several forms

Most Indian pulses are sold in three forms, and each has its own name: whole with the skin (sabut), split with the skin (chhilka), and split without the skin (dhuli). Moong is the clearest example: green whole moong, green-and-yellow moong chhilka, and yellow moong dal are the same bean.

Names that are often mixed up

  • Arhar and toor are two names for the same dal.
  • Sooji and rava are the same thing: coarsely ground wheat.
  • Cornflour and makki ka atta: in India, cornflour means white corn starch. Makki ka atta is yellow maize flour.
  • Besan and chana dal: besan is flour ground from chana dal.
  • Sabudana is made from tapioca (cassava) starch, though it is often called sago.

Questions people ask

Is arhar dal the same as toor dal?

Yes. Arhar and toor (also written tuvar) are names for split pigeon peas.

What is ragi called in English?

Finger millet. In Uttarakhand it is called mandua, and in Maharashtra nachni.

What is besan made from?

Besan is the flour of split Bengal gram, that is, chana dal.

What is the English name of lobia?

Black-eyed beans, also called black-eyed peas or cowpeas.
