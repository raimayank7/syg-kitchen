These are ranges for a stove-top pressure cooker at sea level, with the pressure left to fall by itself. Cookers, flames and the age of the dal all change the time. Start with the lower figure and check.

Minutes, not whistles

Many recipes count whistles, but one cooker whistles every two minutes and another every five. Counting minutes from the first whistle, with the flame turned low, gives the same result in every kitchen. If your family recipe says "three whistles", time your own cooker once and write the minutes down.

Soaking

  • Soak whole beans in plenty of water, at least three times their volume. They swell to more than double.
  • In summer, soak in the fridge or change the water once, so it does not turn sour.
  • Throw away the soaking water of rajma and chana and cook in fresh water.
  • Forgot to soak? Pour boiling water over the beans, cover for an hour, then cook for a little longer than usual.

Rajma must be cooked through

Raw and half-cooked kidney beans contain a natural substance that causes stomach upset. Proper boiling destroys it. Always soak rajma, cook it under pressure until completely soft, and never eat it crunchy.

Questions people ask

How many whistles for rajma?

Whistle counts differ between cookers, so time is safer. Soak rajma overnight, then cook for 20 to 25 minutes on low heat after the cooker reaches full pressure, and let the pressure drop by itself. The beans must crush easily between two fingers.

Why is my dal still hard?

The usual reasons are old dal, hard water, too little water, or salt and sour ingredients added too early. Add tomatoes, tamarind and lemon only after the dal is soft.

Do I have to soak dal?

Split dals such as moong and masoor cook without soaking. Whole beans such as rajma, chana and whole urad need 8 hours of soaking to cook evenly.

Does cooking take longer in the hills?

Yes. Water boils at a lower temperature at height, so open-pan cooking is slower. A pressure cooker helps most there. Add a few minutes to the times on this page.
